Most businesses with digital transformation programmes don't understand why they should want to be digital in the first place. Many of them believe very passionately in being digital and are excited about the opportunities that it provides. In this, they're entirely correct. But if the leadership misses the real reason why being digital is so powerful, then the organisation will inevitably fail to capture the benefits.

<!--more-->

### It's not about customer centricity

Let's start with a bang, and take a shot at one of the modern boardroom's shibboleths. Customer centricity, connecting with your customers, being where your customers are, customer intimacy, or whatever it's called in your neck of the woods is not the main reason to undertake digital transformation. Is it bad to be customer-centric? Not at all! It's vital to have a focus on what your customers need and want. 

The closest thing I have to a motto in business is a particularly elegant turn of phrase by Satya Nadella:

> At the core of our business must be the curiosity and desire to meet a customer’s unarticulated and unmet needs with great technology.

I quite literally couldn't have said it better myself. Meeting a customer's unarticulated and unmet needs obviously qualifies as a customer-centric attitude, and meeting those needs with great technology certainly sounds digital. However, being customer-centric can not only be achieved without being digital, but if transformation is handled hamfistedly, a more digital business could actually be less connected to its customers.
